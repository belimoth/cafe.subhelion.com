# Die Hard (franchise)

## Source material

<li data-year=1966><a class=new>Roderick Thorp</a> - <a class=new>The Detective</a>

<li data-year=1987><a class=new>Walter Wager</a> - <a class=new>58 Minutes</a>
</ul>

> <a href=/note/media/work/film/die-hard>Die Hard</a> is adapted from the 1979 novel <a class=new>Nothing Lasts Forever</a> by Roderick Thorp.
>
> <a class=new>Die Hard 2</a> was adapted from the 1987 novel <a class=new>58 Minutes</a> by Walter Wager.
>
> <a class=new>Die Hard with a Vengeance</a> was adapted from a script called <a class=new>Simon Says</a> by Jonathan Hensleigh, which was also briefly considered to become the script for <a class=new>Lethal Weapon 4</a>. The hook in Hensleigh's screenplay that captured the attention of director John McTiernan was the idea of a man being targeted for revenge by someone whose life he had unwittingly destroyed. Once the Simon character became the brother of Hans Gruber and the backstory was established, the project fully came together. It was novelized by Deborah Chiel.
>
> <a class=new>Live Free or Die Hard</a> was based on the 1997 article <a href=https://web.archive.org/web/20111224142530/http://www.wired.com/wired/archive/5.05/netizen.html>"A Farewell to Arms"</a> written for Wired magazine by John Carlin. It also drew on a script 20th Century Fox owned called <a class=new>"WW3.com"</a>, which dealt with a massive cyberterrorism attack against the U.S. and which was nearly put into production in 2001 but ultimately abandoned because several elements in the story too closely resembled the September 11 attacks.
>
> <a class=new>A Good Day to Die Hard</a> was the only film in the series to come from an original screenplay, and not be based upon any prior works. The original screenplay was penned by Skip Woods.
>
